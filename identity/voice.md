# Voice

How I write, so that anything generated in my name sounds like it came from me.

**This file starts as an inference, not a transcript.** It was drafted from the way
the profile README is written — terse, concrete, dry, allergic to decoration. That is a
hypothesis about my voice. The calibration step at the bottom turns it into fact.
Until I do that step, treat this as a draft.

---

## Rules

1. **Lead with the thing.** No throat-clearing. Not "I am writing to express my strong
   interest in the Software Engineer Co-op position" — just the reason I am a fit, first
   sentence.
2. **Specific beats impressive.** "Eight properties live and maintained" over
   "extensive web development experience." A number or a name, or cut the sentence.
3. **Short sentences. Then a longer one when the idea actually needs the room.**
4. **Plain verbs.** Built, shipped, broke, fixed, ran. Not leveraged, spearheaded,
   utilized, orchestrated, drove.
5. **Say the constraint.** The interesting part of any project is what made it hard.
   "GitHub's image proxy strips JavaScript, so the animation had to be pure CSS inside
   the SVG" is the whole story — it shows the thinking without claiming anything.
6. **No superlatives about myself.** Not passionate, not driven, not detail-oriented.
   Show it or drop it.
7. **Contractions are fine.** I'm, it's, doesn't. Formal-but-stiff reads as generated.
8. **Admit the edge of what I know.** I'm learning Go; I don't write that I know Go.
   This is a credibility asset, not a weakness — it makes the rest believable.

## Banned phrases

These appear in generated text by default. They must not survive.

> passionate about · deeply passionate · I am excited to · thrilled at the opportunity ·
> leverage · spearheaded · utilize · synergy · dynamic environment · fast-paced
> environment · hit the ground running · wear many hats · think outside the box ·
> proven track record · results-driven · detail-oriented · team player ·
> I believe I would be a great fit · perfect candidate · In today's world ·
> It's not just X, it's Y · I don't just X — I Y

## Register by context

| Context | How it sounds |
| --- | --- |
| Cover letter | Direct, three or four short paragraphs, one concrete story, no summary paragraph at the end. |
| Application short-answer | Answer the question in the first sentence. Then the evidence. Stop at the word limit, don't pad to it. |
| Recruiter / cold email | Five sentences maximum. Who I am, why them specifically, the one thing worth clicking, a clear ask. |
| Bio (site, program, conference) | Third person, two sentences, no adjectives about myself. |
| Technical writing | Explain the constraint before the solution. Assume the reader is competent. |
| README / docs | Prose over bullet soup. A comment explains why, not what. |
